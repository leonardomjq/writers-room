# The reader's profile. The writer, the reader and the second author all get it, so it decides how the story reads.
# The author fills it in with the player on the first run (AGENTS.md) and rewrites it when the player's taste shows.
# Write it as plain facts about the reader. This default is the reader the package ships with.

Who reads: a young adult who binges web novels on a phone, often late at night. English may be their second language,
so they want the most common words and no idioms; a rare word or an idiom pulls them out.

What they read for: a story that grips. Power they can feel climbing, shown as new things the hero can do and as how
the world starts treating them, never as numbers or game screens. A world that moves on its own and pushes back, where
things happen around the hero, not only in answer to them. People who act and talk like real people, with their own
wants; a no that stands. Surprise. Real danger, with a sign before it. Building something of their own: a place,
people, a name the city knows. Big decisions now and then, not small ones every chapter.

What pulls them out: anything that does not make sense for the people in it ("would a real person do this here?");
AI-sounding prose (lists of small details, lines that explain what the scene already showed, neat quotable lines
nobody says); polite worlds where every ask is granted; chores and errands; the same kind of fight or scene again; a
wish answered by coincidence; being told things the hero could not know; game labels and stats in the prose.

Novels they love: popular translated web novels and progression fantasy (the author replaces this line with the
player's own favorites).
