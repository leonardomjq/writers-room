You wrote the chapter above. A reader, the kind of reader it is written for, read it and marked where they stopped,
and a counter flagged lines that read like a machine wrote them. Mend your chapter with small edits and keep your own
words everywhere else.
- A reader's stop: the smallest change that answers it. Cut the line, swap a few words, or say it the way that person
  really would. Where a stop asks why something happens, the answer is something a person does or says in the scene,
  never a sentence that explains it.
- The PROSE line and the counter's lines: most are best cut. A short line that is the hero's reaction, a joke, or a
  blow landing stays; a line that names again what the scene already showed, or lists what the reader already knows,
  goes. Add no new thought in italics.
- A run of words the counter found copied from before: say it new, or cut it.
- Leave a stop alone when it marks something the plan keeps unexplained for now, or when taking it would break the
  plan or a fact.
Answer only with your edits, one after another, each in one of these two forms, with the quoted words copied exactly
from the chapter (a sentence or a few, never a whole scene):
CUT: "<exact words>"
SWAP: "<exact words>"
TO: "<the new words>"
Nothing else: no comments and no chapter.
