# A Stranger at the Gate

“Leave. Come back here and I’ll kill you.”

Evan spoke from behind the roof wall. His hands stayed below the edge. He held the lightning inside him until the heat in his arms faded.

Below, Ava Torres kept her palms open.

“I need somewhere to stay.”

“No.”

“There are empty rooms. I saw the building from across the water. I only need a place where I can close a door and sleep.”

She spoke faster toward the end. Then she stopped.

Evan watched her hands.

She knew his name.

He had spent an hour thinking nobody could connect it to his face. Then she had walked to his gate and shouted it up four floors.

That was what mattered. Where she slept did not.

“You heard me,” he said.

Her eyes moved along the roof wall. He lowered his head enough to keep most of his face behind concrete.

“Did anyone come through here before me?”

He did not answer.

She looked back at the empty street. When she faced him again, one hand had closed around the strap of her backpack.

“All right.”

She lowered her other hand slowly.

Evan shifted along the wall to keep her in view. If she reached under her jacket, he wanted to see it before she pulled anything out.

She walked away from the gate.

No running. No last request. Her shoes made little sound on the road.

He followed her with his eyes until she reached the narrow space between a tire shop and the warehouse beside it. She looked up once, checking the walls, then tightened both straps of her pack.

*There.*

He had seen a woman moving across roofs before. He watched for the burst of speed, the jump that should have been too high.

Ava put one shoe against the tire shop wall and caught a metal bracket above her head. She pulled herself up, set her other foot on a pipe, and moved her hand to a crack beside a window.

She tested each hold before trusting it.

The pipe bent away from the wall under her shoe. She stopped at once. Her arms held her while she moved her foot to a narrow strip of brick.

Then she climbed again.

Nothing flashed. Nothing carried her into the air. She worked her way up the side of the building, close to the wall, and brought one knee over the roof edge.

A few seconds later she stood on the tire shop.

She did not look toward him.

She crossed to the warehouse, lowered herself to its roof, and disappeared behind a raised section around a vent. Evan moved to the next corner. From there he saw her once more, crouching beside a low wall with one hand pressed against it.

Then she dropped out of sight.

She did not appear on the next roof.

The buildings beyond the tire shop were packed close together. Low roofs, taller roofs, gaps full of shadow. Even from four floors up, he could not see every way out.

She could be behind the warehouse. She could be sitting below its wall, watching him.

He had no way to know.

Evan stayed where he was.

Following meant leaving the building and entering streets where she might be waiting. He had a locked gate, a concrete wall, and height. He would keep them.

***

He moved away from the edge before standing.

The roof was open except for the stairwell entrance, the vents, and a few pipes. The empty bean can sat where he had left it. A little water remained in the bottle beside it.

He took both into the stairwell.

Metal scraped softly against concrete as he set the can down. He paused and listened.

Nothing below.

He checked the fourth-floor landing, then the turn in the stairs. Every orange door he could see was shut. His own unit was farther down the hall.

He did not go to it.

From inside the stairwell, the building felt much larger. Long halls. Blind turns. Doors he had never opened. It was a good place to hide from the street, but he could not watch all of it from the roof.

He went back up.

The front gate was still chained. The fence ran along both sides of the lot without a break. No one stood beside it.

He checked the river side next.

Sunlight moved over the water. Across it, windows caught the light in tall buildings. Ava had said she had seen this place from there.

He studied them.

There were too many windows.

He stepped back before he had stayed exposed for long.

*She found me once.*

He could not undo that by watching the far bank. He returned to the front of the building and crouched where the wall hid him.

The road approached from the north past a row of warehouses. Anyone coming that way had to cross the junction beside a dead traffic light. From the south, parked trucks blocked part of his view. There was a gap between two of them wide enough for several people.

He watched both.

He checked his watch.

Almost half an hour since Ava had called his name.

When he looked north again, five people were walking past the traffic light.

The first had a rifle.

***

Evan lowered his head.

He had seen a rifle held against a gray hoodie, its long barrel pointing toward the road. Behind the rifle came a heavier man with a shotgun.

Two more guns followed.

*Four.*

He moved a few feet along the wall and looked through a shallow break in its top edge.

Five people. Four armed.

The man in front had a thick neck and close-cut blond hair. He walked quickly, but kept turning to check the others. His rifle moved with him, the barrel crossing the street whenever he looked back.

The shotgun man stayed close. He was broad through the middle and carried his weapon with both hands.

Behind them came a young man and a young woman with pistols. Neither looked much older than twenty. The young man held his pistol down beside his leg. The woman kept hers against her stomach with her other hand under it.

The last one was skinny and unarmed.

He walked close enough to the young man that their shoulders nearly touched.

They stopped outside the lot.

The rifle man looked at the gate, then up the front of the building. Evan stayed below the wall. Through the break in the roof wall, he could see the man standing outside the fence.

“Hello?” the man called.

“We’re looking for someone. A woman. Black jacket, dark hair.”

They had come after Ava.

He had heard no footsteps while she stood below. No voices. These people had arrived from the north, long after she left. They had followed her here, but that did not mean they had heard her.

Evan kept his reply flat.

“She left.”

The rifle man stepped back to see the roof.

“You up there?”

“What do you want?”

The shotgun man moved away from the group, making room between himself and the rifle. Evan shifted back from the edge.

“We need to find her,” the first man said. “Which way did she go?”

“Over the roofs.”

“Which roofs?”

“Lost sight of her.”

The man looked toward the tire shop. His rifle came up slightly as he turned, then dropped again.

“Was she inside?”

“No.”

“We need to check.”

“No.”

The answer stopped him for a moment.

Evan listened to shoes move below. Someone muttered something he could not make out.

“We’ve got people depending on us,” the rifle man said. “She was with our group. Left when things got bad.”

“That’s not what happened, Cal.”

The skinny one.

His voice was quiet, but Evan heard it clearly.

Cal turned toward him.

“Don’t start.”

“You said we were going to talk to her.”

“And we will. When we find her.”

The young woman lowered her pistol to her side. She glanced at the empty road behind them.

Evan drew back until he could no longer see any of them.

Cal called up again.

“She’s scared. She might have told you something. I don’t know. We just need to make sure she isn’t hiding in there.”

“She isn’t.”

“Then let us look.”

“No.”

He stayed still after saying it.

His hand rested against the roof under him. Warm grit pressed into his palm. With lightning in his legs, he could cross the roof in moments. He could get to another edge before anyone below finished turning.

He could not cross four floors of empty air and reach four guns.

He took his hand off the ground.

Below, the gate rattled.

Only once.

Evan moved toward the stairwell, keeping low. If they started climbing, he would reach the stairs before they reached the building. The halls would force them close.

“Locked,” the shotgun man said.

“I can see that,” Cal replied.

The young man with the pistol spoke next.

“She went up, right? He said she went up. We’re losing time.”

“We don’t know she left.”

“He said she did.”

“You believe him?”

Nobody answered.

Evan looked over the wall from farther back.

The shotgun man stood by the gate. Cal was several steps behind him, looking up. The two pistol carriers had stayed near the road. The skinny one watched Cal instead of the roof.

Cal’s face tightened when he caught sight of Evan.

“What’s your name?”

Evan lowered himself behind concrete.

“Leave.”

Silence followed.

He waited for a rifle shot.

A shot would be loud against the building. There would be dust, maybe pieces of concrete. He kept his face down and his mouth closed.

Nothing hit.

“Danny,” Cal said at last. “Call her.”

“She heard you.”

“She’ll answer you.”

Danny did not reply.

“Just call.”

A few seconds passed. Evan heard someone take a breath.

“Ava!”

The name carried along the street and struck the warehouse walls.

Evan watched the patch of sky above the tire shop.

No head appeared.

Danny waited longer than Evan expected. Long enough that the young woman shifted her feet and looked behind them again.

There was no answer.

“She’s gone,” Danny said.

Cal raised his voice.

“If you’re in there, come out. We’re wasting time.”

Evan said nothing.

He had given Cal neither his name nor a glimpse of lightning.

***

The shotgun man walked back to Cal.

“We should go around,” he said.

The young woman shook her head.

“People live here.”

“One person.”

“We don’t know that.”

Cal looked up again. Evan watched him through the break in the wall.

The rifle stayed pointed down now. Cal rubbed his mouth with his free hand, then looked toward the narrow gap where Ava had climbed.

Danny moved closer to the two with pistols.

“She can get a long way while we stand here,” he said.

Cal stared at him.

Danny held his gaze for a moment, then looked away. He did not step back.

A truck door creaked in the wind farther down the street. The young man turned so quickly his pistol lifted. He lowered it when nothing came out.

Cal saw that too.

“Fine,” he said.

He pointed toward the tire shop.

“We check behind there.”

The shotgun man stayed beside him. The others started moving, then stopped when Cal turned back toward the roof.

“If she comes here again, tell her we’re looking for her.”

Evan did not answer.

Cal waited, his chin raised. Then he turned and followed the others.

All five went together.

Evan stayed down until their footsteps had moved away from the fence. He crossed to the corner and watched them reach the gap between the buildings.

The shotgun man looked up at the roof Ava had crossed. Cal pointed farther along the road. Danny stood beside the young woman while the young man checked the alley entrance.

Then they moved on.

No one stayed at his gate.

Evan watched until the last of them disappeared behind the warehouse. He waited for someone to come back around the other side.

The street remained empty.

They had heard Ava’s name at noon. Now four of them carried guns through the city looking for her.

His name had been read three places before hers.

He looked down at the locked gate. The chain hung where he had left it. The concrete under his hands bore no marks.

He had kept them outside without showing them anything.

Evan checked the road once more.

Then he reached into his pocket.

The three blue cores settled into his palm, small and cold against his skin.

He closed his fingers around them without squeezing and turned away from the street.
